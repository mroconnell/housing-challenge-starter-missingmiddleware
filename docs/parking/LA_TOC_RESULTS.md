# Parking: Los Angeles transit-program projects, results

Written 2026-10-28 by the parking conductor. Plan: [LA_TOC_PLAN.md](LA_TOC_PLAN.md), committed before reading, with changes logged. Fetch: `scripts/fetch_parking_la_toc.py`. Coded projects: `reference/parking_la_toc_projects.csv`. Source: CEQAnet (State of California), City of Los Angeles Notices of Exemption.

## Answer

**When the parking rule was cut, most projects still built parking, but less than the old rule required.** On average they built about two-thirds of the old requirement. That is close to Buffalo's 79%.

## Sample

| Step | Count |
| --- | ---: |
| City of Los Angeles filings in the search | 2,507 |
| Mentioning the transit program and homes | 23 |
| Unique projects (repeat filings removed) | 19 |
| With a parking count | **12** (645 homes) |

All 12 were filed in 2024–2026, after the state's AB 2097 removed parking minimums near major transit. Most of them still cite the transit program (TOC), whose own minimum is 0.5 spaces per home or less.

## Reading

- **This matches Ryan's description.** Developers keep building some parking. Family-size units keep nearly all of it, while studio and one-bedroom buildings drop about half.
- **It fits Buffalo** (79% of the old minimums) **and the scenario's illustration** of developers building about four-fifths of today's minimums.
- **Limits:**
  - 12 projects, all recent;
  - descriptions written by applicants;
  - the program also raised allowed density, so the homes count is not a parking-only effect.
- **The tool does not change.**
