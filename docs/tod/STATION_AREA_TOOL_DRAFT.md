Info text (Little response): "From our own lot-by-lot data on 16 US places that upzoned land near rail or rapid
transit. For each, we compared building near the reform's stations with building near similar stations without a
reform, before and after. A model trained on these places, tested by hiding one place at a time, beat simple rules
only within a quarter mile of stops. Places that upzoned a small area near their stations gained; places that upzoned
most of the land near every station (Los Angeles, San Diego, Chicago 2022, Honolulu) gained about nothing so far.
Wisconsin's law is broad, so the model puts it with them: about 40 extra homes over five years, between −160 and 340.
If the broad programs' affordable-housing requirements are what held them back, it would be about 210. Caution: near
Milwaukee's new streetcar, building rose by a similar amount with no rezoning at all."

Why A: the 171 line rests on one study; the new line is our own measurement and lands in the same place. The central
line keeps its meaning (where new homes go), and the screen gains a measured "caused by the law" figure.
