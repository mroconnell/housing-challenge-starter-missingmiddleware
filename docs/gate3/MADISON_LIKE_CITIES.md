# Madison-like cities in statewide-ADU-law states

> **Used in the adopted model (2026-10-25, AQ-042):** Madison now follows the WA+OR median path in this document (0.452, 0.542, 0.601, 0.799, 1.076 permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year in 2027–2031) in every scenario. Its addition is 135 permits over 2027–2031. The model figures quoted below (typical-state path 0.83, 1.03, 1.07, 1.30; Madison plateau 1.75) are the 2026-10-24 model. The adopted model's statewide central is 0.77, 0.95, 0.99, 1.19, 1.19 permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year, and Madison's pooled plateau (now a sensitivity) is 1.63. See the [Gate 3 packet](README.md).

Owner question, 2026-10-25: have Madison-like cities in states with a statewide ADU law climbed to the rates the model predicts?

**All numbers are descriptive.** A rate that rose after a state law is *associated with* the law. Every city also changed its own rules, interest rates moved, and there is no comparison group, so none of this estimates the law's effect.

**Units used throughout.** "Rate" = ADU permits in the city in a year ÷ the city's one-unit detached homes (ACS 2020–2024, B25024_002) × 1,000, written as *permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year*. Where a table gives "permits / homes", the first number is the count of ADU permits and the second the home count. Medians are the middle city's rate (not weighted); "pooled" = all cities' permits ÷ all cities' homes × 1,000.

## Plain summary

- **Question.** Have Madison-like cities in statewide-law states climbed to the rates the model predicts? The model's typical-state path is 0.83, 1.03, 1.07, then 1.30 permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year in years 1, 2, 3 and 4+ after the law. Madison's own model plateau is 1.75. Madison today runs at 0.18.
- **The cities.** The definition (section 0) was fixed before any outcome was read: 20,000–120,000 single-family homes, median home value $250,000–$500,000, not the state's largest city.
  - **Washington (5):** Spokane, Spokane Valley, Tacoma, Vancouver, Yakima.
  - **Oregon (4):** Eugene, Salem, Medford, Gresham.
  - **Rhode Island (2):** Warwick, Cranston.
  - **Maine, Vermont:** no city qualifies (none has 20,000 single-family homes).
  - **California (15),** shown only as a mature-industry comparison: Fresno, Bakersfield, Stockton, Modesto, San Bernardino, Lancaster, Palmdale, Visalia, Clovis, Victorville, Redding, Indio, Hesperia, Chico, Citrus Heights.
- **Answer: no, not yet.** By the pre-registered rule the evidence **contradicts** the claim for years 1–3. It **cannot yet test** years 4 and later, where only four Oregon cities have data.
  - **Years 1–3 (9 Washington and Oregon cities):** the median city ran at **0.45, 0.54 and 0.60** permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year. That is about half the model's 0.83, 1.03 and 1.07.
  - **Pooled, the same 9 cities:** 0.70, 0.94 and 0.61 (241, 327 and 213 permits ÷ 346,919 homes). Tacoma's 2019 reform drives most of the pooled years 1–2.
  - **Years 4–5 (Oregon only, 4 cities):** the median reached **0.80** and **1.08**. That fits a slower climb toward about 1. It is not the 1.30 the model has from year 4, and four cities are too few to settle it.
  - **Against Madison:** every year's median is at least 2.5 times Madison's 0.18. The cities do rise above Madison's level. They do not reach the model's path on its timetable.
- **Why the test is weaker than it looks.**
  - **Own reforms.** Six of the nine Washington and Oregon cities made their own ADU reforms in the same window (Tacoma 2019, Spokane 2021–22, Yakima 2019–23, Salem's 2017 rules and 2019 fee waiver, Eugene's 2019 fee credit, Medford's 2020 fee cut). The three without one (Spokane Valley, Vancouver, Gresham) ran lower: medians 0.33, 0.43 and 0.11 in years 1–3.
  - **Washington law not yet binding.** Washington's years 1–3 are mostly before cities had to comply. Spokane, Spokane Valley and Yakima are due 2026-12-31; Spokane Valley still requires owner occupancy and an extra parking space.
  - **Includes new-house ADUs.** These counts include ADUs built with a new house; the Wisconsin measure excludes them (about 5%, see below).
- **California's Madison-like cities.** These 13 cities exclude the two holdouts. Their median was **0.00–0.41** permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year in years 1–5 after the 2017 law. It was **0.96–1.42** in years 6–9 (2022–2025), after the 2020 state package; pooled, 1.12–1.73.
  - So even California's mature ADU industry took 6–9 years and a second round of state law to bring Madison-like markets to about 1.3–1.4.
  - That is far below California's statewide plateau of 3.88.
- **ADUs built together with a new house.** This replaces the arbitrary 80% adjustment. Under owner decision AQ-039, they count when the lot had a home (teardown-rebuild) and not when it was vacant.
  - **Vacant-lot share.** The median city's share of all ADUs that were vacant-lot new-house ADUs is **4.9%**. That covers 17 cities split with demolition records; the range is 0.0%–25.3%, and pooled it is 7.9% of ADUs.
  - **The adjustment this implies** for comparison-state counts is about ×0.95, not ×0.8 (applying it is the conductor's or owner's call).
  - **Total new-house share,** before the split: median 5.3% (18 cities), pooled 8.6%.
  - **Outliers, shown separately:** Seattle (36.2%) and Austin (65.7%) are condo-driven, per the owner. Lancaster, CA (57.0%) is subdivision-driven: builder-installed ADUs on new tract homes.
- **Did reform raise vacant-lot ADUs too?** Yes, where the test can be run at all.
  - **Portland, 2010 fee waiver:** vacant-lot ADUs per 100 new vacant-lot houses rose ×11. Existing-lot ADUs per 1,000 homes rose ×7.8.
  - **California Madison-like cities, 2020 state package:** both rose sharply.
  - **Where existing-lot ADUs fell** (Portland after 2022, Minneapolis after 2021), vacant-lot ADUs fell too.
  - **Denver** had none in either period.
  - **So** vacant-lot ADUs move with reform rather than being a fixed builder habit. Counts are small and subdivision cycles confound the comparison, so this is weak evidence.

## 0. Definition and analysis plan (written 2026-10-25 before any city outcome was looked at)

This section was written and saved before any city's ADU permits were read. Later sections may add results, but nothing in this section is changed after outcomes are seen; any change is listed as a deviation in section 7.

**A Madison-like city** is a Census 2020 incorporated place (place class code C*) that meets all of:

1. **Size:** 20,000 to 120,000 one-unit detached homes (ACS 2020–2024 5-year, B25024_002, inclusive). Madison has 53,292.
2. **Market:** median value of owner-occupied homes from $250,000 to $500,000 (ACS 2020–2024 5-year, B25077_001, inclusive). Madison's is $372,900.
3. **Not the state's largest metro core city:** each state's largest city is excluded by name: Seattle (WA), Portland (OR), Los Angeles (CA), Providence (RI), Portland (ME), Burlington (VT). Suburbs and second cities of those metros stay in. (Wording fixed after the size and value screen and before any outcome was read: the first draft named only Seattle, Portland OR and Los Angeles and was ambiguous about Providence, which passes the size and value tests.)
4. **States:** Washington and Oregon (main test); Maine, Vermont and Rhode Island (added if any city fits); California (mature-industry comparison, never in the main median).

**Optional flag (not a filter):** `university_or_capital` = the city is a state capital, or contains a university campus of at least 10,000 students (IPEDS institutional characteristics file, size category 10,000–19,999 or 20,000+, degree-granting four-year). Results are reported for all Madison-like cities (main) and for the flagged subset (secondary).

**Outcome measure:** ADU permits per 1,000 one-unit detached homes per year = (ADU permits in the city in the year) ÷ (city's ACS 2020–2024 one-unit detached homes) × 1,000. The same ACS denominator is used for every year.
- Washington: OFM "Permitted Accessory Dwelling units" by city and estimate year (EY = April 2 of the previous year to April 1).
- Oregon: DLCD HB 4006 annual summaries, ADUs permitted, calendar year, cities over 10,000.
- California: HCD Annual Progress Report Table A2, ADU units with building permits issued, calendar year.
- Maine, Vermont, Rhode Island: city-level official counts if any exist.

**Years since law** (same coding as POOLED_MODEL.md section A): year 1 is the first (nearly) full year with the statewide law in force.
- Washington HB 1337 (effective 2023-07-23): year 1 = EY2024, year 2 = EY2025, year 3 = EY2026. "Before" = EY2019–2023 (5 years). Most Washington cities did not have to comply until 2025-06-30 or later; this is recorded per city.
- Oregon SB 1051 (effective 2018-07-01): 2018 = law year (shown, not used), year 1 = 2019 ... year 4 = 2022; 2023 = year 5 only if the city reported. No before year exists in DLCD data.
- California (first strong law 2017-01-01): year 1 = 2017; APR city data begin 2018 = year 2.

**Local reform:** for each city, any city-adopted ADU ordinance change in the window (before window start through the last data year) is recorded with its adoption or effective date and source, so state-driven and city-driven rises can be told apart. A city whose own reform falls inside the after window is flagged `local_reform_in_window`.

**Summary statistics (pre-registered):**
- Main: the unweighted median city rate by year since law, across Washington and Oregon Madison-like cities (plus Maine, Vermont, Rhode Island if any qualify and have data). Also reported: the pooled rate (sum of permits ÷ sum of homes) and the median for cities without a local reform in the window.
- Compared with: the model's typical-state path 0.83, 1.03, 1.07, 1.30 permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year (years 1, 2, 3, 4+); Madison's plateau 1.75; Madison's current 0.18.
- California Madison-like cities are reported as a separate median path (years 2–9).

**Verdict rule (pre-registered):**
- **Supports** the claim that Madison-like cities climb to about 1.0–1.3 if the main median is at or above 1.0 in the latest year since law that has at least 5 cities with data, and that year is year 2 or later.
- **Contradicts** it if the main median is below 0.8 in year 3 or later with at least 5 cities with data.
- **Cannot yet test** otherwise, including when fewer than 5 cities have data in year 2 or later.
- Washington years before a city's compliance date are counted under "years since law" (as POOLED_MODEL.md does) but the verdict is also stated for post-compliance years only.

**New-house share:** where city permit data identify ADUs permitted together with a new house, share = ADUs permitted with a new house ÷ all ADUs permitted (units, same city and window). Sources: reference/other_cities_period_rates.csv (Seattle, Portland, Austin, Denver, Minneapolis: units_incl_new_house − adu_units), plus any other city data found. The median is the unweighted median of city shares.

## 3. City reforms vs state law (separating the two)

Research of 2026-10-25 on official city pages, ordinances and minutes (V = verified) or news (L = source_lead). Full text is in `reference/madison_like_cities.csv` (`local_own_reforms`, `state_compliance_changes`, `reform_note`).

- **Tacoma:** the clearest city-driven rise.
  - Ord. 28576 (effective 2019-05-01, V) allowed detached ADUs and dropped owner occupancy and parking four years before HB 1337.
  - The rate went from 0.54 (EY2019–23) to 1.85 and 2.52 in EY2024–25, then fell to 0.93 in EY2026, the first year under Home in Tacoma Phase 2 (2025-02-01). Whether activity moved into the new middle-housing types was not checked.
- **Spokane:** city-driven.
  - Fee waiver from 2021-11 and removal of owner occupancy 2022-08-07 (V). The owner's note that the 2022 "Building Opportunity and Choices for All" interim drove ADUs is not quite right: that ordinance (C36232) was middle housing only. The ADU changes were C36225.
  - The jump came in EY2023 (46 permits, 0.70), before the state law existed.
- **Yakima:** own reforms 2019–2023; state compliance still pending.
- **Vancouver:** liberal since 2017 (owner occupancy and parking removed). Its in-window change was the state-compliance interim ordinance of 2024-09-09. Its rate barely moved: 0.51 → 0.67, 0.74, 0.60.
- **Spokane Valley:** no liberalizing change (owner occupancy, a deed restriction and an extra parking space still apply). Its rate fell: 0.18 → 0.33, 0.15, 0.11.
- **Oregon:** every city adopted SB 1051 compliance code in 2018–2021. Three also cut or waived system development charges (SDCs, one-time infrastructure fees) on their own (Eugene 2019, Salem 2019, Medford 2020). Those three rose. Gresham, which kept an extra-parking requirement, fell to 0.00–0.09 by years 3–5.
- **Rhode Island:** neither city made its own change. Both rises (Warwick 0.08 → 0.50; Cranston 0.19 → 0.28) are state-driven.
- **California:** post-2017 local ordinances in these cities are state-compliance ordinances. Own-initiative reforms were not separately researched.
- **Washington deadlines** were changed by SSB 5558 (2025): ADU rules now take effect with the periodic plan update. Tacoma's was due 2025-06-30, Vancouver's 2025-12-31, and Spokane, Spokane Valley and Yakima's 2026-12-31. So for 3 of 5 Washington cities, none of years 1–3 is after compliance.

## 5. Verdict

Pre-registered rule (section 0): **contradicts**. The main median is 0.60 permits per 1,000 single-family homes per year in year 3, below 0.8, with 9 cities with data. The post-compliance-only version gives the same answer: 0.61 in year 3, 6 cities. Recorded in [`reference/madison_like_verdict.csv`](../../reference/madison_like_verdict.csv).

In plain terms:
- **Years 1–3 contradict the claim.** Madison-like cities in Washington and Oregon did not climb to about 1.0–1.3 in years 1–3. The typical one ran at 0.45–0.60, about half the model's 0.83–1.07. The three without their own reform ran lower still (0.11–0.43).
- **Years 4+ cannot yet be tested.** Only four Oregon cities have years 4–5. Their median, 0.80 then 1.08, is consistent with a slow climb to about 1, driven by the two cities that waived or cut fees (Salem, Medford). Four cities are too few.
- **California agrees on timing.** Its Madison-like markets took until years 6–9, and a second state law, to reach a median of 1.0–1.4.
- **Madison's 1.75 plateau** is above every median and every pooled rate observed in any group and year (the highest is California's year-9 pooled 1.73).
- **What the evidence does support:** Madison-like cities under a statewide law run well above Madison's 0.18 (2.5–6 times). Cities that also cut fees or removed owner occupancy reach about 1–2.

**What would change this.**
- OFM EY2027–28 (Washington's first fully post-compliance years for Spokane, Spokane Valley and Yakima).
- Oregon 2024–2025 city tables (now with Portland State University).
- Rhode Island 2026.

### 6b. Owner decision AQ-039: teardown-rebuild vs vacant lot

**Rule.** A new-house ADU counts when the lot previously had a home (teardown-rebuild) and does not count when the lot was vacant.

**Method:**
- **Teardown:** a new house (and its ADU) is a teardown-rebuild when either:
  - a dwelling demolition or wrecking permit on the same parcel was issued from 2 years before to 1 year after the new-house permit (Portland also matches on street address); or
  - the new-house record itself reports demolished units (Minneapolis `dwellingUnitsEliminated`; California APR `DEM_DES_UNITS` on the same APN within ±1 report year).
- **Vacant lot:** otherwise.
- **Unclassified:** the record has no parcel or address, or the 2-year look-back starts before the demolition data begin.

**Sources:**
- Portland: residential demolition permits, PortlandMaps layer 126, single-family dwelling demolitions from 2000.
- Denver: demolition permits layer 318, "Wreck" class from mid-2015.
- Minneapolis: CCS wrecking permits from Dec 2016.
- California: APR demolished-units field, report years 2018+.

**Limits:**
- **Portland:** a lot split after demolition gives the new house a new parcel id. The address match catches some of these, but teardowns may still be undercounted.
- **California:** the APR demolition field is sparsely filled. Only 101 of 48,264 new single-family houses in these 15 cities are flagged as teardowns. Those cities are largely greenfield, but the vacant share there is close to an upper bound.
- **Not split:**
  - Seattle and Austin, which are shown separately per the owner.
  - Tacoma, which has only an aggregate count; its total new-house share (5.4%) is an upper bound.

Counts by city, so either rule (exclude vacant-lot ADUs, or count them as 1 unit each) can be applied without redoing the work. File: [`reference/madison_like_new_house_lot_split.csv`](../../reference/madison_like_new_house_lot_split.csv).

## 7. Did reform raise vacant-lot ADUs? (owner request, 2026-10-25)

Two series per city and year (file [`reference/madison_like_new_house_lot_by_year.csv`](../../reference/madison_like_new_house_lot_by_year.csv); comparison [`reference/madison_like_vacant_lot_reform_test.csv`](../../reference/madison_like_vacant_lot_reform_test.csv)):
- **(a)** vacant-lot new-house ADUs per 100 new single-family houses permitted on vacant lots;
- **(b)** ADUs on lots that already had a home (existing-lot plus teardown-rebuild new-house ADUs) per 1,000 single-family homes per year.

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | ---: | --- | --- | ---: |

**Notes on (b):**
- Homes are fixed at the ACS count: Portland 160,807, Denver 145,528, Minneapolis 84,075, California pooled 618,449 (579,762 without Lancaster).
- In (b), "before"/"after" ADUs are totals over the window; the rate divides by the number of years.
- Minneapolis (a) counts houses only from late 2018, when the 2-year look-back is first covered by wrecking data; earlier ones are unclassified.

**Interpretation.**
- **Vacant-lot ADUs moved with reform.**
  - Where existing-lot ADUs jumped (Portland's 2010 fee waiver; California's 2020 package), vacant-lot ADUs per new vacant-lot house jumped as much or more.
  - Where existing-lot ADUs fell (Portland after the 2018 fee deadline and RIP; Minneapolis after 2021), vacant-lot ADUs fell too.
  - That argues against "a steady builder practice the law didn't change": builders add ADUs to new houses when ADUs are cheap and allowed. A statewide law would likely raise vacant-lot ADUs in Wisconsin as well.
- **The test is weak:**
  - **Small counts:** Denver has 0 vacant-lot ADUs in both periods, and Minneapolis 5 and 4.
  - **Short or partial windows:** Denver after = 1 year; California before = 2 APR years that undercount ADUs.
  - **Subdivision cycles:** vacant-lot house counts swing with tract openings (Portland 4,355 → 2,227 houses; Lancaster's tract-map ADUs).
  - **Fees confound:** Portland's 2010 change was a fee waiver that applied to new-house ADUs too, which is not the same as a rights change.
  - **Undercounted teardowns:** demolition matching undercounts teardowns (lot splits, sparse California demolition field), which pushes some teardown ADUs into "vacant".
- **The counting rule is unchanged** (vacant-lot ADUs excluded). This only informs the owner's later decision.

## 8. Deviations from section 0

1. **Largest-city wording.** Rule 3 was reworded after the size/value screen and before any outcome was read (see the note in rule 3). Effect: Providence excluded.
2. **University flag tightened after outcomes were seen.** It now requires Carnegie 2021 basic class 15 or higher, which drops community colleges that carry the four-year IPEDS level (Bakersfield College, Modesto Junior College, Antelope Valley College). It changes no Washington or Oregon city and no reported median. The California flag was only descriptive.
3. **Year coding.** OFM estimate years are used as calendar-equivalent years; EY2024 (Apr 2023–Mar 2024) is year 1, as in POOLED_MODEL.md. California APR report years are used as calendar years.
4. **Rhode Island in the main median.** It has only year 1 (2 cities) and could not change the verdict. It is reported separately rather than mixed into the year-1 Washington/Oregon median. The pre-registered "main" set (WA, OR, plus ME/VT/RI if they qualify) would add Warwick (0.50) and Cranston (0.28) to year 1. That would give 11 cities with a median of 0.45, unchanged.
5. **New-house work added at the owner's request** (condo/subdivision flags; AQ-039 split; vacant-lot reform test). Not pre-registered.
