# California comparison study: results

Gate 3, work package A. Runs the pre-registered plan [CA_COMPARISON_PLAN.md](CA_COMPARISON_PLAN.md) (approved by the owner as G2-8) on the Gate 2 inputs ([CA_COMPARISON_DATA.md](../gate2/CA_COMPARISON_DATA.md)). This file is generated by `scripts/run_ca_study.py` from its outputs; `--check-doc` fails if it differs. **All results are descriptive comparisons around the statewide reform waves.** Groups are defined from pre-period outcomes and there is no untreated comparison state, so differences are *associated with* the waves; they are not estimates of causal effects. Every number that could feed the Wisconsin model is computed on non-holdout jurisdictions only; holdout jurisdictions are used once, in the hold-out test below, and no all-data refit has been done.

## Plain-language summary

- **Places that had no ADUs before 2020 started producing them after the 2020 state laws.** The 73 non-holdout jurisdictions with zero ADU permits in every year 2017–2019 (mostly small places, median 5,190 one-unit detached homes) reached **1.23 [0.70 to 2.01] ADU-type second homes per 1,000 one-unit detached homes a year** in 2023–2025 (jurisdiction-clustered bootstrap 95% interval in brackets).
- **They ramped up over about four years:** 10% of that 2023–2025 level in 2020, 53% in 2021, 58% in 2022 and 85% in 2023; the rate was still rising in 2025, so the "plateau" is the pre-registered 2023–2025 average, not a demonstrated ceiling.
- **Already-permissive places also rose (spillover).** The top quarter by 2017–2019 rate went from 3.06 to 7.31 per 1,000 (×2.39 [1.94 to 3.91]). Without the City of Los Angeles the permissive group went from 1.77 to 5.42 (×3.05). The middle group went from 0.32 to 2.39.
- **Across all grouped non-holdout jurisdictions** the rate went from 0.12 a year in 2014–2016 to 1.16 in 2017–2019 and 3.84 [2.63 to 5.30] in 2023–2025.
- **The owner's ADU-share model:** ADU-type second homes went from about 1% of new units (BPS total plus ADUs outside BPS) in 2014–2016 to 23–25% in 2023–2025 (the range is the unidentified share of ADUs already inside BPS, 0 to 0.5). Applied to Wisconsin's 2023–2025 BPS totals it implies about 3.75 ADU-type second homes per 1,000 Wisconsin one-unit detached homes a year (bootstrap 2.76–4.81; 3.27–4.39 if the overlap differs between the states).
- **The hold-out test matched at the group level.** Using only the non-holdout curve, the 90 grouped holdout jurisdictions' 2020–2025 ADUs were predicted at 18,391 against 17,417 observed (-5%); 18 of 18 group-year rates fell inside the (wide) predictive interval. Individual places miss by much more (median absolute error 1.07 per 1,000 a year).
- **Candidate Wisconsin inputs (not adopted):** central `y` ≈ 1.23 per 1,000 (restrictive-group plateau); upper `y` ≈ 3.84 (all-group plateau) or 3.75 (share model); ramp ≈ 0.10, 0.53, 0.58, 0.85 of plateau in years 1–4; spillover multiplier for already-permissive places ≈ ×2.4 (×3.1 without Los Angeles). California has higher rents and land values and a longer reform history; these transfer to Wisconsin only as stated assumptions.

| 3 (pre 2017–2019, post 2020–2025) | 3.06 [1.56 to 4.65] | 3.12 [2.01 to 3.87] | 1.57 [1.30 to 1.88] | 0.86 [0.50 to 1.38] | -2.26 [-3.13 to -1.05] |

Reading: in every wave the permissive group's absolute rise was larger than the restrictive group's, so the DiD is negative. This does not show that restrictive places responded less: already-active markets added more permits per home in absolute terms, and the restrictive group's rise is from zero. The groups are selected on pre-period outcomes, so some regression toward the mean is built in (permissive places selected on high values tend to fall back, restrictive places selected on zeros can only rise); this biases the DiD toward zero from the restrictive side and away from zero on the permissive side, and is a further reason to read it descriptively. Wave 1 and wave 2 share a clock and a pre-period, so wave 2 is cumulative from 2014–2016. Without the City of Los Angeles the DiD is -0.25 in wave 1, -0.65 in wave 2 and -1.82 in wave 3: Los Angeles accounts for most of the permissive group's early rise.

**Transfer caveats (apply to every number above):**

- California rents and land values are much higher than Wisconsin's, which raises the return on a second home; the same law may produce fewer permits per home in Wisconsin.
- California's 2020 package came after 2017 and 2018 laws, local ordinances since 1982, and a statewide ADU industry; restrictive places in wave 3 already had three years of statewide ADU marketing. A first Wisconsin law starts from less.
- HCD ADU counts still include legalizations, ADUs on multifamily lots and with new houses that the notes do not identify (identified ones, about 1%, are excluded), and the 2012–2017 compilation includes all of them.
- Junior ADUs (interior conversions within the house) are included, as in Wisconsin's observed counts; under AQ-030 the benchmark gives interior conversions no reform uplift, so the transferred `y` slightly overstates the benchmark-form response.
- California's 2020 package also capped fees, paused owner occupancy and set an exemption floor; the Wisconsin benchmark is silent on these (AQ-022, AQ-025).
- The restrictive group is small places (median about 5,000 homes); large Wisconsin cities are better matched by the middle group.
- The study has no untreated comparison state; rises after each wave also reflect California's housing market (rents, rates, the 2017–2018 wildfires and rebuilds in some places).
- Single-family-to-duplex conversions (Wisconsin-only component) have no California counterpart here; any reform effect applied to them is an assumption.

## 11. Interpretations of the plan and deviations

Where the plan left a detail open, the choice below was fixed before any group result was computed. None changes a pre-registered group, window or check.

- **Group mean** is the homes-weighted rate (permits ÷ homes, pooled over the group's observed jurisdictions); the unweighted mean of jurisdiction rates is also published. The plan's Los Angeles check implies a size-weighted mean.
- **"At least 3 permits"** is read as the pre-period total.
- **Top quarter** is by rank among the wave's jurisdictions with complete pre-periods, ties at the cut included.
- **Year counting:** 2020 is year 1, so the year-4-to-6 plateau is 2023–2025 (2026 is not yet observed).
- **Excluding 2020** drops 2020 from post windows and event tables; groups and the plateau are unchanged.
- **Text exclusions** use keyword rules (section 1); the plan asked for exclusions "where the record identifies them" without a method.
- **Holdout grouping** uses the non-holdout permissive cutoff, so the holdout test uses nothing estimated on holdout data.
- **Added after the first run, as extra descriptive columns only:** the "all grouped" line, the increment-share ramps for the middle and permissive groups, and the bootstrap of A ÷ B. The candidate upper-path ramp (middle group) was picked after seeing them, and is labelled a candidate.
- **Rerun after a bug fix:** the first run counted missing jurisdiction-years as zero inside the wave statistics (not in the event tables). It was fixed and everything, including the holdout test, was rerun with unchanged methods; the holdout test was not altered in response to its result.
- **Bootstrap** resamples jurisdictions within each pre-registered group (groups held fixed).
- **Share-model sample** excludes county remainders flagged in step 0 (S6) because their BPS may include cities.
- **Deviation:** the plan mentions an event-study chart; this package publishes the tables (and the CSV behind them) only.
- **Not done here:** the Madison vs Sun Prairie check, the unknown-rules holdout and later-state tests (other work packages); the statewide BPS view is in step 0.
